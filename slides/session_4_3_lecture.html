What problem are we solving?

Learn how to use AI to compare options with simple numbers: give the AI the facts and the formula, check the calculations, and decide with more than one criterion.

The situation

The problem

Ara works part-time in a small company's support office. She has written three useful prompts, but she has only two hours this week to test and polish one of them. Her root problem is choosing the prompt that will help most. Her idea is to ask ChatGPT. She asked, "Which of my three prompts is best to polish?" ChatGPT chose the quarterly report outline: "It is the most strategic and impressive." But that prompt is used once a month, saves the least time, and has the highest risk of number errors. The AI judged by how impressive the task sounds because the prompt gave it no facts and no criteria.

Today's goal and a three-step method

Learn how to use AI to compare options with simple numbers: give the AI the facts and the formula, check the calculations, and decide with more than one criterion.

Step 1

Give the AI the facts and the formula

Paste the data and define how to calculate the value of each option.

Step 2

Calculate, then check

Ask for a table of results and check at least one row with a calculator.

Step 3

Decide with more than one criterion

Ask for a choice that considers time saved, reuse, and risk, and label every estimate.

Part 1Session 4.3

Five decision words you need first (continued)

Before you ask an AI to compare options, you must know these words. Each example uses Prompt A.

Key idea

Reuse ease and error risk

Two scores from 1 to 3. Reuse ease 3 means the prompt works with little change. Error risk 3 means a mistake would be serious, such as a wrong number in a report.

Key idea

Estimate

A number based on a guess, not a measurement. "Half the time" is an estimate, so the result must be labeled as an estimate.

Part 3Session 4.3

How the three-step method fixes the choice

The instructor runs Poor Prompt 1 and Improved Prompts 1 and 2 below in front of the class and compares the answers.

Key idea

Step 1 fixes the missing-facts problem

With frequency and minutes, the AI can calculate value instead of judging by the task name.

Key idea

Step 2 fixes the trust problem

Checking one row with a calculator takes one minute, and multiplication mistakes are common.

Key idea

Step 3 fixes the one-number problem

Time saved is important, but reuse and risk matter too. A fast prompt with a high risk still needs careful human checks.

What the AI often answers

A typical result

"The quarterly report outline. It is the most strategic task and shows the highest-level skills."

Why it is weak

Reasons

  • It gives no frequency or minutes, so the AI judges by how important the task name sounds.
  • It gives no criteria, so "best" can mean anything.
  • The result picks the prompt that saves the least time.

Improved prompt 1

Steps 1 and 2. Copy this prompt exactly.

Improved prompt 1 - copy and send
AI Chat
You
I want to choose one of my prompts to polish. Use only the facts below.

Prompt A: daily support summary; 10 minutes per task without AI; used 20 times a month.
Prompt B: weekly meeting recap; 20 minutes per task; used 4 times a month.
Prompt C: quarterly report outline; 60 minutes per task; used once a month.
Estimate: with a polished prompt, each task takes half the time.
Use these formulas:
- Time saved per use = minutes per task / 2
- Time saved per month = time saved per use x uses per month

Make a table with these columns: prompt, time saved per use, uses per month,
time saved per month. Show the calculation for Prompt A so I can check it.
Label the results "Estimate." Do not recommend anything yet.
Message AI...

Why it works

Reasons

Improved prompt 2 - copy and send
AI Chat
You
I checked your table: A saves 100 minutes a month, B 40, and C 30.
Add two scores from 1 to 3:
Reuse ease: A 3, B 3, C 1. Error risk: A 1, B 2, C 3 (3 = most serious).

Recommend one prompt to polish first, using all three criteria:
time saved per month, reuse ease, and error risk.
Give the reason in two sentences and one caveat in one sentence.
Message AI...
Why it works

Reasons

  • It gives the checked numbers, so the recommendation uses correct values.
  • Adding reuse and risk scores makes the decision more than one number.
  • Asking for a caveat keeps the recommendation honest about estimates and risk.
  • The length limits make the answer quick to read and easy to check.
