Problem firstSession 2.1

What problem are we solving?

Root problem: the lunch line at Mina's bubble tea shop is too long.

Root problem

The lunch line is too long

  • Many students arrive at lunch at the same time.
  • Staff must prepare five different drinks, so each order takes longer.
  • Customers wait too long for their drinks.
Mina's idea

Remove one drink from the menu

  • Fewer drinks means faster preparation and a shorter line.
  • But the shop must not lose much profit.
  • So which drink should go?
What went wrong

AI chose the wrong drink

  • Mina asked ChatGPT: "Which drink should I remove?"
  • ChatGPT: "Remove Mango Fruit Tea, because it sells the fewest cups."
  • But Mango Fruit Tea earns the most money per cup. The question did not tell the AI what matters.

Goal and methodSession 2.1

Today's goal and a three-step method

Learn how to give business data to an AI tool so it reads the data correctly.

Step 1

Explain the data

  • What each column means.
  • What unit it uses.
  • How to calculate the measure you care about.
Step 2

Get a table, then check one row

  • Ask the AI to show results in a table.
  • Check one row yourself with a calculator.
Step 3

Ask for a decision

  • State your goal.
  • Ask for facts and guesses separately.
  • Then ask for the recommendation.

Part 1 | Business wordsSession 2.1

Price, ingredient cost, and revenue

Before you ask an AI to analyze sales, know what the numbers mean. Example: one cup of Brown Sugar Milk Tea.

Word 1

Price

What one customer pays for one cup.

$5.00

Word 2

Ingredient cost

What the shop pays for the tea, milk, sugar, and cup used to make one cup.

$2.40

Word 3

Revenue

All the money customers paid.

Revenue = cups sold x price

420 x $5.00 = $2,100.00

Revenue is not the money the shop keeps. Ingredients still have to be paid for.

Business AI Tools | Lecture NotesSession 2.1 | Slide 5
Part 1 | Business wordsSession 2.1

Gross profit: the money left after ingredients

This is the measure Mina needs. It is the key idea of this session.

Word 4

Gross profit

Gross profit per cup = price - ingredient cost

$5.00 - $2.40 = $2.60 per cup

Weekly gross profit = cups sold x gross profit per cup

420 x $2.60 = $1,092.00

Be careful

Gross profit is not final profit

  • Gross profit subtracts only ingredient cost.
  • It does not subtract rent or staff wages.
  • So it is not the same as the money Mina takes home.
  • This data can support gross profit, and nothing more.

Part 3 | The fixSession 2.1

How the three-step method fixes the question

When a question gives no rule for deciding, AI often uses the easiest number to see. The question was incomplete.

Step 1 fixes

The definition problem

  • The AI now knows that "profit" means gross profit.
  • It knows how to calculate it.
Step 2 fixes

The trust problem

  • One shown calculation lets you check the math in one minute.
  • AI tools can make arithmetic mistakes. Always check at least one row.
Step 3 fixes

The decision problem

  • The AI knows the goal: shorter line, least profit lost.
  • It must say what it is guessing, such as how long each drink takes to make.

What the AI often answers

A typical result

"Remove Mango Fruit Tea because it has the lowest sales."

Reasons

  • It gives no rule for deciding, so the AI uses the most visible number: cups sold.
  • It does not state the goal: a shorter lunch line while keeping as much profit as possible.

Improved prompt 1

Steps 1 and 2. Copy this prompt exactly.

I run a small bubble tea shop. Below is one week of sales data.

What each column means:
- cups_sold: number of cups sold this week
- price_per_cup_USD: what one customer pays for one cup, in US dollars
- ingredient_cost_per_cup_USD: cost of the ingredients for one cup, in US dollars

Use these definitions:
- Revenue = cups_sold x price_per_cup_USD
- Gross profit per cup = price_per_cup_USD - ingredient_cost_per_cup_USD
- Weekly gross profit = cups_sold x gross profit per cup
Gross profit does not include rent or staff wages.

Make a table with these columns: drink, cups_sold, revenue,

Sort the table by weekly gross profit, from highest to lowest.
Show the calculation for the Taro Smoothie row so I can check it.
