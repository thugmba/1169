What problem are we solving?

Learn how to use AI to study a process problem: give the AI the process and the log, separate evidence from guesses, and plan a small test before spending money.

The situation

The problem

Sora manages a small online-shop warehouse. On Monday, 18 of 30 orders were shipped late, and customers started to complain. Her root problem is that late orders keep happening this week, and she does not know why. Her idea is to ask ChatGPT. She asked, "18 of 30 orders were late. Why? How do I fix it?" ChatGPT answered, "Your staff are too slow. Hire two more pickers and buy new scanners for $1,200." But Sora's own log shows that the two scanners were not charged at opening this week, and the charging checklist was blank. The AI guessed a cause and an expensive fix because the prompt gave it no evidence.

Today's goal and a three-step method

Learn how to use AI to study a process problem: give the AI the process and the log, separate evidence from guesses, and plan a small test before spending money.

Step 1

Give the AI the process and the log

Paste the steps and the data. Tell the AI to use only them.

Step 2

Separate evidence from guesses

Ask for late rates, the evidence, the unknowns, and possible causes in separate lists. Check the rates yourself.

Step 3

Plan a small test

Before buying or hiring, test the most likely cause with one measure, one time period, and one limitation.

Part 1Session 3.2

Five process words you need first

Before you ask an AI about a process problem, you must know these words. Each example uses Sora's warehouse.

Key idea

Process step

One action in the work. The warehouse has four steps: Pick, Scan, Pack, and Courier.

Key idea

Late rate

Late orders as a percent of all orders. This Monday: 18 / 30 = 60%.

Key idea

Evidence

A fact in the log. Example: "This week, 0 of 2 scanners were charged at opening."

Part 1Session 3.2

Five process words you need first (continued)

Before you ask an AI about a process problem, you must know these words. Each example uses Sora's warehouse.

Key idea

Possible cause

An explanation that fits the evidence but is not proven. Example: "Uncharged scanners slowed the Scan step."

Key idea

Small test

A short, low-cost check of one possible cause. Example: sign the charging checklist every day for one week and compare the late rate.

How the three-step method fixes the answer

The instructor runs Poor Prompt 1 and Improved Prompts 1 and 2 below in front of the class and compares the answers.

Key idea

Step 1 fixes the missing-evidence problem

With the process and the log, the AI can compare this week with last week.

Key idea

Step 2 fixes the blame problem

Separate lists keep facts, unknowns, and guesses apart, so no worker is blamed without evidence.

Key idea

Step 3 fixes the expensive-fix problem

A one-week checklist test costs almost nothing. Sora should run it before spending $1,200.

Poor prompt 1 - do not use
AI Chat
You
On Monday, 18 of 30 orders at our warehouse were late. Why? How do I fix it?
Message AI...
What the AI often answers

A typical result

"Late orders usually mean your staff are too slow or understaffed. Hire two more pickers and buy new, faster scanners."

Why it is weak

Reasons

  • It gives no process and no log, so the AI uses general reasons for late orders.
  • It asks "why" and "how to fix" in one step, so the AI jumps to a solution without evidence.
  • The result blames workers and suggests spending money on a guessed cause.

Poor prompt 2 - do not use
AI Chat
You
Our warehouse is slow. This week our two scanners were not charged.

Message AI...
What the AI often answers

A typical result

A list of "accountability steps," such as warnings for the closing staff, without any check of the data.

Why it is weak

Reasons

  • It asks the AI to blame a person, but the data does not say who forgot to charge the scanners.
  • It treats one possible cause as a proven fact.
  • Blame does not fix the process. A missing checklist step is a process problem.

2. List: Evidence, Unknowns, and Possible causes, in three separate lists.

Do not blame individual workers. Do not recommend purchases or hiring.

Each part of the prompt matters.

Why it works

Reasons

  • It gives the process and the full log, which fixes the missing evidence in Poor Prompt 1.
  • Asking for late rates turns "many orders were late" into numbers you can compare.
  • Three separate lists keep facts, unknowns, and guesses apart.
  • "Do not blame individual workers" prevents the problem in Poor Prompt 2.
  • "Do not recommend purchases or hiring" stops the expensive fix before the cause is tested.

Improved prompt 2 - copy and send
AI Chat
You
I checked your rates: last week 6.7% (10 of 150), this week 48.9% (44 of 90).
The most likely possible cause is uncharged scanners.

Plan a one-week test of this cause. Include:
- The one change we will make
- The one measure we will record each day
- What result would support the cause, compared with 6.7% and 48.9%
- One reason the result can suggest the cause but not prove it
Keep the plan under 120 words. Do not include any purchase.

Part A - copy and send
AI Chat
You
Continue with the same process and log.
Here is new data: This week Thu 30 orders, 3 late; Fri 32 orders, 2 late.
On both days, 2 of 2 scanners were charged and the checklist was signed.
Calculate the late rate for Thursday and Friday together and compare it
with last week (6.7%) and with Monday to Wednesday (48.9%).
Does this support the possible cause? Give one fact with numbers,
and one reason the data does not prove the cause.
Keep the answer under 80 words.
Message AI...
Check the answer

Expected result

Thursday and Friday had 5 late orders out of 62, so the late rate is 5 / 62 = 8.1%. This is close to last week's 6.7% and far below 48.9%, so it supports the possible cause. It does not prove it, because two days is a short period and other things may have changed, too.
