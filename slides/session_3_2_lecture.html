Lesson explanationSession 3.2

Understand the idea and apply it

Work through the explanation before comparing prompts or starting the exercise.

Learn

Describe the process problem with a count

  • The supplied observation is that 18 of 30 Monday orders were late. That is a rate of 60% for that sample. It describes the scale of the problem on Monday; it does not show what caused the delays or prove that other days had the same rate. Keep the time period and denominator attached to the number.
Learn

Use cause questions as hypotheses, not conclusions

  • Two scanners were found uncharged and the charging checklist was blank for three days. These facts suggest a possible equipment-readiness issue, but they do not prove it caused all 18 delays. A simple "why" chain helps the team ask what happened earlier in the process. Each proposed link should be marked observed, supported, or still unknown. Do not turn a plausible story into an established root cause.

Lesson explanationSession 3.2

Learn

Test one manageable cause

  • If the team suspects scanner readiness, use a one-week charging checklist and record whether scanners are ready at shift start, orders picked per hour, and late orders. Compare the same measures with a recent baseline if available. If scanner readiness improves but lateness does not, weaken or revise the hypothesis. The purpose is to learn which process change helps, not to assign blame.
Apply

Three-step method

  • State the measurable problem; build a short cause chain and label evidence versus unknowns; choose a low-risk test and a measure.
