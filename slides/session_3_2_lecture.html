The problem

Map a simple process, distinguish evidence from assumptions, and propose a small test of a likely process cause.

Concept and methodSession 3.2

Concepts for this problem

Keep each concept tied to the practical decision.

Understand

Core concepts

  • A bottleneck is the step that currently limits flow; find it with timing or queue evidence, not a guess.
  • The 5 Whys asks why a problem occurred repeatedly, but each answer is a hypothesis until checked.
  • Focus on process conditions and safeguards, not blame. One observation may not explain all late orders.
Apply

A simple method

  • State the measurable problem; build a short cause chain and label evidence versus unknowns; choose a low-risk test and a measure.

Compare examplesSession 3.2

A clearer, safer prompt

Keep the source, task, constraints, and output check visible.

Use

Improved example 1

Use only the facts below. List what is observed, what is unknown, and up to three possible causes of late orders. Mark each cause as a hypothesis; do not assign blame.
Use

Improved example 2

For the hypothesis that uncharged scanners slowed picking, propose a one-week test. Specify the daily measure, comparison, and a result that would weaken this hypothesis. Do not assume it explains all 18 late orders.
