What problem are we solving?

Learn how to use AI for fair hiring documents: give the AI the real duties, link every requirement to a duty, and use the same interview question and scoring guide for every candidate.

The situation

The problem

Yuna is a manager at a small online shop. She needs a junior customer support assistant. Her root problem is that her job ad did not work: only 4 people applied in three weeks. Her idea is to ask ChatGPT to write a better ad. She asked, "Write a job ad for a junior customer support assistant." The new ad asked for a bachelor's degree, three years of experience, a "young and energetic" person, and a "native English speaker." Only 2 of its 6 requirements match the real duties of the job, and some could unfairly turn away good applicants. The AI wrote a typical ad because the prompt did not include the real duties.

Today's goal and a three-step method

Learn how to use AI for fair hiring documents: give the AI the real duties, link every requirement to a duty, and use the same interview question and scoring guide for every candidate.

Step 1

Give the AI the real duties

Paste the duties and the required and not-required lists. Tell the AI to use only them.

Step 2

Link every requirement to a duty

Ask the AI to name the duty after each requirement. Remove any requirement with no duty.

Step 3

Use one question and one scoring guide for everyone

Ask the same job-related question to every candidate and score only what the answer shows.

Part 1Session 3.3

Five hiring words you need first

Before you ask an AI to write hiring documents, you must know these words. Each example uses Yuna's support job.

Key idea

Duty

A task the person will do in the job. Example: answer customer email questions.

Key idea

Requirement

A skill the person must have to do a duty. Example: clear writing, because the person answers emails.

Key idea

Job-related

Connected to a duty. Age, accent, and "energy" are not job-related, so they must not be requirements.

Five hiring words you need first (continued)

Before you ask an AI to write hiring documents, you must know these words. Each example uses Yuna's support job.

Key idea

Structured interview

Every candidate gets the same questions in the same order, so the answers can be compared fairly.

Key idea

Scoring guide

A short table that says what an answer looks like at each score. Example: 1 = guesses an answer; 3 = says they will follow up, records the case, and asks the supervisor.

What the AI often answers

A typical result

A polished ad that asks for a bachelor's degree, two or three years of experience, "excellent communication skills," and a "fast-paced, energetic" personality.

Why it is weak

Reasons

  • It gives no duties, so the AI copies requirements from typical job ads.
  • It does not say what is not required, so the AI adds a degree and years of experience.
  • The result can turn away good applicants, which was Yuna's problem in the first place.

We are hiring a Junior Customer Support Assistant for a small online shop.
Use only the job facts below.

Duties:
- Answer customer email questions (about 60% of the time)
- Record case details in a spreadsheet (about 30%)
- Flag unresolved cases to the supervisor (about 10%)
Required: clear writing; careful recordkeeping.
Not required: a specific degree; years of experience (training is provided).
Write a job ad under 150 words with: a two-sentence role summary,
the duties, and the requirements.
After each requirement, write the duty it supports in brackets.
Do not add a degree, years of experience, age, or personality words.

Why it works

Reasons

Improved prompt 2

Improved prompt 2 - copy and send
AI Chat
You
Your ad is correct. Now write one interview question for the duty
"Flag unresolved cases to the supervisor." Every candidate will get the same question.

Then write a scoring guide with three levels:
1 = does not meet the skill, 2 = partly meets the skill, 3 = meets the skill well.
For each level, describe what the candidate says or does in the answer.
Score only job-related actions. Do not score accent, confidence, or appearance.
Message AI...
Why it works

Reasons

  • One question for every candidate makes the answers comparable.
  • The question is linked to a real duty, so it tests the job.
  • Each level describes what the candidate does, so two interviewers give the same score.
  • "Do not score accent, confidence, or appearance" keeps the interview fair.
