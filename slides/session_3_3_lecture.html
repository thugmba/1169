Problem firstSession 3.3

What problem are we solving?

Learn how to use AI with a grading rule: build a job description step by step until the AI grades it A, grade how well you fit it by adding your facts one at a time, and then improve one gap at a time until you have an ordered plan to reach grade A.

The situation

The problem

Leo is a business student. He wants a job as a Junior Customer Support Assistant at a small online shop after he graduates. His root problem is that he does not know what this job really needs, or how close he is to it. His idea is to ask an AI chat. He typed, "Here is my resume. How well do I match a customer support job? Give me a percentage. I am hard-working, good at Excel, and I like people." The AI answered, "About 85%. You are a strong fit!" It gave no reasons. When Leo graded himself with a clear rule and real evidence, his fit was only 56% (grade C), and the AI's "good at Excel" was worth 0 points because he had no evidence for it.

Goal and methodSession 3.3

Today's goal and a three-step method

Learn how to use AI with a grading rule: build a job description step by step until the AI grades it A, grade how well you fit it by adding your facts one at a time, and then improve one gap at a time until you have an ordered plan to reach grade A.

Step 1

Write the grading rule first

Give the AI a rule with clear criteria and grades (A to E). The AI grades; you only check it.

Step 2

Improve the prompt until the grade is A

Build the job description, grade it in a new chat, add what failed, and grade it again.

Step 3

Add facts, then fix one gap at a time

Grade your fit after each fact. Then ask the AI for the one gap with the most lost points, add a planned fact, grade again, and repeat.

Part 1Session 3.3

Six words you need first

Before you build a job description, you must know these words. Each example uses Leo's support job.

Key idea

Job description (JD)

A document that lists the duties of a job and the skills it needs. Companies use it to hire, and you can use it to plan your studies.

Key idea

Duty

A task the person does in the job. Example: answer customer emails, about 60% of the time.

Key idea

Requirement

A skill needed for a duty. Example: clear writing in English, because the person answers emails. A requirement with no duty should be removed.

Business AI Tools | Lecture NotesSession 3.3 | Slide 8
Part 1Session 3.3

Six words you need first (continued)

Before you build a job description, you must know these words. Each example uses Leo's support job.

Key idea

Weight

How important a requirement is. Must-have = 2 points, Nice-to-have = 1 point. Example: 3 Must-haves and 2 Nice-to-haves = 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 8 maximum points.

Key idea

Evidence

A fact with a number or a result. "I am good at Excel" is not evidence. "I made a 50-row table with COUNTIF in a class project" is evidence.

Key idea

Match %

Points earned / maximum points x 100. Example: 4.5 / 8 x 100 = 56%.

The two grading rules (key points)

You do not grade anything yourself. You paste these rules into the AI, and the AI grades. Rule 1 grades the job description with five Pass or Fail criteria.

Key points

What the table shows

  • Rule 1 grade: 5 passes = A, 4 = B, 3 = C, 2 = D, 0 or 1 = E.
  • Rule 2 grades your fit: each requirement gets 0 (no evidence), 0.5 (some evidence), or 1 (clear evidence). Points = weight x score.
  • Rule 2 grade: 85% or more = A, 70-84% = B, 55-69% = C, 40-54% = D, below 40% = E.
  • Always grade in a new chat. In the same chat, the AI often gives its own job description a high grade.

Leo's grades go up step by step (key points)

The instructor runs Leo's case in front of the class. His job description went from E to D to B to A with the four prompts below. Then he added his facts one at a time.

Key points

What the table shows

  • Leo guessed B before the grading. His real grade was C.
  • "I like traveling" matches no requirement, so the grade did not change. More facts do not always mean a higher grade.
  • Improvement ladder, one gap per round: Round 1 fixes Basic Excel (2 lost points) and raises him to 6.5 / 8 = 81%, grade B. Round 2 fixes recordkeeping (1 lost point) and raises him to 7.5 / 8 = 94%, grade A.
  • The order matters: if he first fixed "Knowing when to ask for help" (0.5 lost points), he would reach only 5 / 8 = 63%, still grade C. Asking "What should I improve?" gives a long list of tips with no order and no effect on the grade.

What the AI often answers

A typical result

A typical ad with a bachelor's degree, 1-2 years of experience, an "energetic team player," and no share of time. Rule 1 grade: E.

Why it is weak

Reasons

  • It gives no company or duties, so the AI copies a typical ad.
  • The requirements are not linked to duties and have no weights, so you cannot grade yourself against them.
  • It allows unfair words such as "energetic" and a degree the job does not need.

Why it works

Reasons

  • It keeps what already passed (duties and links), so the grade cannot go down.
  • The weights make the requirements countable: 3 Must-haves and 2 Nice-to-haves = 8 points.
  • The "do not include" lines remove the unfair requirements, so criterion 4 passes. Rule 1 grade: B.

Grading prompt - copy and send
AI Chat
You
You are a strict reviewer of job descriptions. Grade the job description below with this rule only.
Criteria (Pass or Fail):
1. Duties: 3 to 5 concrete duties, each with a share of time, and the shares add up to 100%.
2. Linked: every requirement names the duty it supports.
3. Weighted: every requirement is marked Must-have (weight 2) or Nice-to-have (weight 1).
4. Fair: no age, gender, nationality, appearance, or personality words, and no degree or years of experience unless a duty needs it.
5. Checkable: every requirement says what evidence proves it.
Grade: 5 passes = A, 4 = B, 3 = C, 2 = D, 0 or 1 = E.
For each criterion, write Pass or Fail and quote the words from the job description that show it. Then give the grade.
Job description:
[paste the AI's job description here]
