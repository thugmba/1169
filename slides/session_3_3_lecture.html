Learn

Start a job ad with the work

  • The fictional junior support role includes responding to customer emails, recording cases accurately, and escalating unresolved issues. A requirement should connect to one of these duties. "Can record a customer issue clearly" is observable; "dynamic superstar" is vague and does not tell an applicant what work they would do.
Learn

Separate necessary skills from assumptions

  • A skill is supported when it follows from a real duty, such as clear writing for email replies or careful note-taking for case records. Do not add age, personality, degree, or experience requirements unless the supplied role facts justify them. Plain language helps applicants understand what they will be asked to do.

Lesson explanationSession 3.3

Understand the idea and apply it

Learn

Score the same evidence consistently

  • Ask each candidate the same job-related question and use the same short scoring guide. A score should reflect stated steps, such as acknowledging the email, checking the case record, and escalating when unresolved. Do not score accent, confidence, or similarity to the interviewer. If evidence is missing, ask the same follow-up rather than filling the gap with an assumption.
Apply

Three-step method

  • Extract duties and necessary skills; draft plain-language requirements; create a shared question and simple evidence-based scoring guide.

Use

Improved example 1

Use only the duties and requirements below. Draft a concise junior job ad
with three duties and three observable skills. Do not add degree, experience,

Use

Improved example 2

Write one structured interview question about handling an unresolved customer email.
Give a 1-3 guide: 1 = no clear steps; 2 = describes a reasonable response;
3 = explains response, accurate case notes, and when to escalate.
Apply the same guide to every candidate.
