Situation

The problem

Sales can rise while the money left after direct costs falls. A manager needs to explain the change without confusing sales, direct costs, and overhead.

Target

Learning goal

Calculate gross profit and gross margin, check the arithmetic, and write a short evidence-based briefing.

Case material

Practice data (fictional shop)

  • Year A: revenue $20,000; cost of goods sold (COGS) $12,000; operating expenses (OPEX) $5,000. Year B: revenue $22,000; COGS $14,300; OPEX $5,200. COGS is the direct cost of goods sold; OPEX is the cost of running the business outside those direct costs.

Concept and methodSession 3.1

Concepts for this problem

Keep each concept tied to the practical decision.

Understand

Core concepts

  • Gross profit = revenue - COGS. Gross margin = gross profit / revenue x 100.
  • Gross margin describes the share of sales left after direct product costs; it is not the same as final net profit.
  • Separate reported figures (facts) from explanations of why they changed (hypotheses). These figures alone do not identify the cause.
  • Use a calculator or spreadsheet to verify arithmetic; use AI to organize and explain checked figures.
Apply

A simple method

  • Calculate each year's gross profit and margin; compare the figures and label facts versus possible explanations; write a brief that names one question to investigate next.

A clearer, safer prompt

Keep the source, task, constraints, and output check visible.

Use

Improved example 1

Using only the four figures for Year A and Year B below, calculate gross profit and gross margin. Show the formula and arithmetic. Do not call gross profit net profit.
Use

Improved example 2

Use these checked results: Year A gross profit $8,000 and margin 40%; Year B gross profit $7,700 and margin 35%. Write a 100-word manager brief: state two facts, one possible explanation labeled as unverified, and one follow-up question. Do not invent a cause.
