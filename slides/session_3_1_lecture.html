Learn

Keep revenue, gross profit, and margin distinct

  • Revenue is sales before costs. Gross profit = revenue - cost of goods sold (COGS), the direct cost of the items sold. Gross margin = gross profit / revenue x 100. Margin describes the share of revenue left after direct product costs; it is not net profit because operating expenses are still outside this calculation.
Learn

Work through the supplied two-year figures

  • Year A gross profit is $20,000 - $12,000 = $8,000 and margin is $8,000 / $20,000 = 40%. Year B gross profit is $22,000 - $14,300 = $7,700 and margin is $7,700 / $22,000 = 35%. Revenue rose by $2,000, while gross profit fell by $300 and gross margin fell by 5 percentage points. Verify each formula in a spreadsheet or calculator before explaining the result.

Lesson explanationSession 3.1

Understand the idea and apply it

Work through the explanation before comparing prompts or starting the exercise.

Learn

Explain the change without inventing a cause

  • The figures establish what changed, but not why. Higher direct costs may be worth investigating, but these four figures do not identify which cost, product, or decision caused the change. Label an explanation as a hypothesis and ask for the next useful evidence, such as item-level costs or sales mix. A short manager brief should separate checked facts from the follow-up question.
Apply

Three-step method

  • Calculate each year's gross profit and margin; compare the figures and label facts versus possible explanations; write a brief that names one question to investigate next.
