# Business AI Tools: Effective ChatGPT Communication

## Introduction to the Course in English
Prompt engineering is crafting precise, context-rich inputs to large language models such as Google Antigravity to generate useful, accurate, and relevant outputs. It is important because well-designed prompts unlock the full potential of AI, enabling businesses to automate analyses, drive insights, and enhance decision-making.

By the end of this course, students will be able to explain the fundamental principles and components of prompt engineering and how large language models interpret and generate responses. They will develop the skills to design clear, specific, and context-rich prompts tailored to key business-management scenarios such as marketing, finance, operations, and human resources. Through hands-on practice and iterative refinement, students will learn to evaluate the quality of AI outputs, identify biases or errors, and implement strategies for continuous prompt optimization. They will integrate prompt-driven workflows into real-world business processes, confidently automating routine analyses and report generation. Finally, students will demonstrate responsible and ethical use of AI by recognizing potential risks, ensuring data privacy, and establishing guardrails to mitigate bias in their applications.

### Module 1: Mastering ChatGPT Fundamentals

#### Session 1.1: Getting Started with ChatGPT in Business
- **Duration:** 3 Hours (180 minutes)
  - *Hour 1 (50 min):* Comprehensive Concept Lecture & Live Demonstration
  - *Hour 2 (50 min):* Guided Hands-On Lab (Comparing prompt depths with real reviews)
  - *Hour 3 (50 min):* Structured Worksheet Exercise (Prompt Specificity Scorecard)
- **Goal:** Learn what ChatGPT can and cannot do, understand how it generates text, and build safety habits for daily office tasks.
- **Lecture Content (Hour 1 — 50 Minutes):**
  - **Part 1: How LLMs Actually Work (15 min):**  
    *The Predictive Autocomplete Analogy:* Most business professionals mistakenly believe ChatGPT searches an encyclopedia database or "thinks" like a human. In reality, Large Language Models predict the next most likely word token based on statistical patterns learned from vast training datasets. When an LLM generates text, it calculates probability distributions across billions of parameters. Because it generates text probabilistically rather than retrieving hard records, it produces fluent language, but has no inherent sense of truth.
  - **Part 2: ChatGPT vs. Google Search — The Strategic Distinction (15 min):**  
    *Decision Framework:* Teach students when to search vs. when to prompt.
    - *Use Traditional Search (Google):* For verifiable historical facts, live stock quotes, statutory regulations, telephone numbers, and cited scientific research.
    - *Use Generative AI (ChatGPT):* For drafting executive correspondence, translating tone (e.g., technical jargon to layperson English), summarizing unformatted text, and brainstorming strategic angles.
    - *The Modern Business Hybrid Workflow:* (1) Gather verified facts from primary company sources -> (2) Feed those verified facts into ChatGPT to draft and format the communication.
  - **Part 3: The Hallucination Problem & Enterprise Risk (10 min):**  
    *What is a Hallucination?* Plausibly sounding, highly confident statements generated by AI that are completely false.  
    *Case Study — Air Canada Lawsuit (2024):* An Air Canada customer service chatbot invented a non-existent bereavement refund policy. A customer relied on the chatbot's advice and sued when the refund was refused. The Canadian tribunal ruled Air Canada legally liable for its chatbot's hallucination.  
    *Managerial Golden Rule:* AI is a brilliant junior copywriter, but a terrible factual auditor. Never approve an AI-generated document without personally verifying dates, names, and calculations.
  - **Part 4: Instructor Live Demonstration Script & Discussion (10 min):**  
    *Step 1 (Naive Request):* Type `"Write an apology email to an angry customer whose delivery was late."` Note the empty platitudes (*"We value your business"*) and lack of actionable next steps.  
    *Step 2 (Engineered Prompt):* Type a structured prompt specifying Role (*"Customer Support Lead"*), Context (*"4-day courier delay on subscription coffee"*), Task (*"Draft 3-paragraph email"*), Constraints (*"Acknowledge delay in sentence 1; offer $10 credit code [COFFEE10]; explain backup carrier policy in 2 bullets; under 150 words"*).  
    *Discussion Question:* *"Why does setting a specific persona (like 'Senior Operations Lead') improve AI output quality?"* (Answer: It narrows the model's vocabulary and probability distribution toward professional domain terminology).
- **Public Data Source:** [PyCaret Open Amazon Customer Reviews Dataset (CSV)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pycaret/pycaret/master/datasets/amazon.csv) — Real open customer reviews and ratings.
- **How to Practice:**
  1. **Step 1:** Open the public dataset link and copy 2 real customer reviews (one positive review and one complaint).
  2. **Step 2:** In ChatGPT, paste the reviews and enter a basic prompt: *"Write a customer service reply to these reviews."* Notice how generic the reply sounds.
  3. **Step 3:** In a new chat window, enter an engineered prompt: *"You are the Customer Support Lead for an online retail brand. Write a polite, empathetic, 3-paragraph reply apologizing for the issue, offering a replacement order, and explaining our quality check process. Tone: Warm and professional."*
  4. **Step 4:** Compare Output A and Output B side by side. Record your evaluation in the Prompt Specificity Scorecard.
- **What to Expect:** See how giving clear background information produces much more relevant, professional, and practical answers.
- **Exercise (Prompt Comparison Scorecard):** Fill out an A/B Scorecard comparing the two answers on usefulness, accuracy, and professional tone (score 1 to 5), with one short sentence explaining the difference.

#### Session 1.2: Structuring Your Prompts for Success
- **Duration:** 3 Hours (180 minutes)
  - *Hour 1 (50 min):* Comprehensive Concept Lecture & Live Demonstration
  - *Hour 2 (50 min):* Guided Hands-On Lab (Deconstructing corporate press releases)
  - *Hour 3 (50 min):* Structured Worksheet Exercise (4-Box Architecture Form)
- **Goal:** Master the 4-part prompt formula to consistently generate executive-ready business deliverables.
- **Lecture Content (Hour 1 — 50 Minutes):**
  - **Part 1: The Cost of Unstructured Prompts in Business (15 min):**  
    When employees give ambiguous prompts, ChatGPT generates an "average" response that requires multiple cycles of manual editing. Every cycle wastes valuable business hours. Prompt architecture provides the model with the exact structural boundaries it needs to produce workplace-ready output on the very first try.
  - **Part 2: The 4-Box Prompt Architecture Framework (15 min):**  
    Teach the 4 modular building blocks:
    1. **Role / Persona:** Establishes the authoritative viewpoint, tone, and professional vocabulary (e.g., *"Senior Product Marketing Director"*).
    2. **Business Context:** The operational backdrop, target customer, problem being solved, and market conditions.
    3. **Task Directive:** An unambiguous command verb stating the exact deliverable (e.g., *"Draft a launch announcement memo"*).
    4. **Output Format Template:** The exact schema, headings, bullet counts, and sections required.
  - **Part 3: Delimiters and Modular Prompt Design (10 min):**  
    Why delimiters matter: Using symbols like `###`, `---`, or `"""` clearly separates instructions from raw data. This prevents the model from confusing text to analyze with commands to execute, establishing clean prompt hygiene.
  - **Part 4: Instructor Live Demonstration Script & Discussion (10 min):**  
    *Live Demo:* Extract 4 facts from a corporate press release on PR Newswire. Type a 4-box modular prompt live on screen into ChatGPT to draft an internal company briefing. Show how defining the Output Format Template ensures the AI never outputs conversational pleasantries like *"Sure, here is your email!"*.  
    *Discussion Question:* *"If you omit the Output Format Template, what default formatting mistakes does ChatGPT typically make?"* (Answer: It produces rambling introductory paragraphs, inconsistent bullet points, and excessive sign-offs).
- **Public Data Source:** [PR Newswire Public Business Press Releases](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/) — Free, real-world corporate product launch announcements and press releases.
- **How to Practice:**
  1. **Step 1:** Open the PR Newswire page and click on any recent product launch announcement.
  2. **Step 2:** Read the article and extract 4 key facts: company name, product name, main customer problem solved, and target audience.
  3. **Step 3:** In your worksheet, build your prompt by explicitly filling in the 4 boxes: `[Role]`, `[Business Context]`, `[Task Directive]`, and `[Sample Output Template]`.
  4. **Step 4:** Copy your complete 4-part prompt into ChatGPT. Review the output to confirm whether it followed your exact output structure without extra conversational filler.
- **What to Expect:** Receive an answer that matches professional business formats without needing to rewrite it multiple times.
- **Exercise (4-Box Prompt Form):** Complete a 4-box form defining the Role, Context, Task, and Format for your chosen announcement.

#### Session 1.3: Writing Clear, Step-by-Step Instructions
- **Duration:** 3 Hours (180 minutes)
  - *Hour 1 (50 min):* Comprehensive Concept Lecture & Live Demonstration
  - *Hour 2 (50 min):* Guided Hands-On Lab (Enforcing 5 strict rules on management guides)
  - *Hour 3 (50 min):* Structured Worksheet Exercise (Rule Checklist Matrix)
- **Goal:** Eliminate ambiguous phrasing and enforce strict negative and positive constraints on AI outputs.
- **Lecture Content (Hour 1 — 50 Minutes):**
  - **Part 1: The Power of Constraints in Executive Writing (15 min):**  
    Executives do not read long paragraphs. They scan for metrics, decisions, and deadlines. Generative AI naturally defaults to wordy, passive-voice prose. To make AI useful for leadership communication, managers must apply strict constraints that eliminate unnecessary words.
  - **Part 2: Positive vs. Negative Constraints (15 min):**  
    - *Positive Constraints (What to Include):* Format (e.g., *"Exactly 3 numbered bullet points"*), Length (e.g., *"Under 120 words"*), Target audience (e.g., *"Written for a first-time store manager"*), Required data (e.g., *"Must cite the $50 budget limit"*).
    - *Negative Constraints (What to Forbid):* Explicitly banning corporate fluff, clichés, and buzzwords (*"Do NOT use words like 'synergy', 'game-changer', 'delve', 'testament', or 'seamless'"*).
  - **Part 3: Calibrating Tone for Stakeholders (10 min):**  
    Demonstrate how tone constraints alter vocabulary:
    - *CFO Tone:* Quantitative, concise, risk-focused, zero adjectives.
    - *Frontline Employee Tone:* Empathetic, direct, step-by-step, actionable.
  - **Part 4: Instructor Live Demonstration Script & Discussion (10 min):**  
    *Live Demo:* Take a dense 400-word business operations policy from the SBA guide. Prompt ChatGPT without rules (producing 300 words of generic prose). Then prompt with 5 strict constraints. Count the words live on screen and show how negative constraints force crisp, high-density business communication.  
    *Discussion Question:* *"Why is it harder for AI to write under 100 words than over 500 words?"* (Answer: Compressing ideas requires prioritization and omission, whereas expanding text aligns with the model's natural statistical verbosity).
- **Public Data Source:** [US Small Business Administration (SBA) Business Guide](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/manage-your-business) — Free public business operating guides and management guidelines.
- **How to Practice:**
  1. **Step 1:** Open the SBA guide link and copy 2 paragraphs on managing business operations or staff.
  2. **Step 2:** In ChatGPT, test an unconstrained prompt: *"Summarize this text for my store."* Observe that the summary is too long and unstructured.
  3. **Step 3:** Write a constrained prompt: *"Summarize the text above under these 5 strict rules: (1) Maximum 120 words total, (2) Exactly 3 numbered bullet points, (3) Written for a first-time store manager, (4) Do NOT use words like 'synergy' or 'game-changer', and (5) Include one clear action step to take today."*
  4. **Step 4:** Run the constrained prompt. Audit the output line by line and mark Pass or Fail for each rule in your Rule Checklist Matrix.
- **What to Expect:** ChatGPT will follow your rules closely, giving a concise answer without unnecessary extra text.
- **Exercise (Rule Checklist Matrix):** Check ChatGPT's response with a simple Pass/Fail checklist for each of your 5 rules.

#### Session 1.4: Step-by-Step Problem Solving
- **Duration:** 3 Hours (180 minutes)
  - *Hour 1 (50 min):* Comprehensive Concept Lecture & Live Demonstration
  - *Hour 2 (50 min):* Guided Hands-On Lab (Strategic evaluation of retail automation)
  - *Hour 3 (50 min):* Structured Worksheet Exercise (Step-by-Step Logic Table)
- **Goal:** Guide ChatGPT to think through complicated business problems step by step and spot hidden assumptions.
- **Lecture Content (Hour 1 — 50 Minutes):**
  - **Part 1: The Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Mechanism (15 min):**  
    When asked a complex business question directly (e.g., *"Should we open a second store branch?"*), an LLM immediately guesses the most probable conclusion, often giving a superficial "Yes". However, when instructed to *"Think step-by-step through costs, market demand, and risks before answering"*, the model generates intermediate tokens that ground its final decision, reducing reasoning errors by up to 70%.
  - **Part 2: Uncovering Hidden Business Assumptions (15 min):**  
    AI models are trained on general internet literature and often assume idealized conditions: 100% customer adoption, zero employee turnover, and perfect supply chains. Business students must learn to interrogate AI reasoning: *"What assumptions are you making about our labor costs and customer habits?"*
  - **Part 3: Multi-Stage Decision Frameworks (10 min):**  
    Teach students to structure complex problem-solving prompts in 4 logical stages:  
    1. Financial Feasibility -> 2. Operational Capacity -> 3. Customer Friction -> 4. Synthesis & Risk Mitigation.
  - **Part 4: Instructor Live Demonstration Script & Discussion (10 min):**  
    *Live Demo:* Present a dilemma: *"Should a local supermarket replace 4 cashiers with self-checkout kiosks?"*  
    Prompt 1 (Direct): Gives a generic positive recommendation.  
    Prompt 2 (CoT): Instructs AI to evaluate theft risks, initial equipment depreciation, and elderly customer frustration step-by-step. Show how the final recommendation shifts toward a phased, hybrid pilot program.  
    *Discussion Question:* *"Why should business leaders never accept a strategic recommendation without seeing the underlying reasoning steps?"* (Answer: The recommendation might be based on unrealistic operational assumptions that could lead to financial losses).
- **Public Data Source:** [MIT Sloan Management Ideas Articles](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter) — Free open-access business strategy articles and case studies on new technology adoption.
- **How to Practice:**
  1. **Step 1:** Open the MIT Sloan link and browse a recent article on technology adoption (e.g., self-checkout or AI in retail).
  2. **Step 2:** Formulate a strategic prompt in ChatGPT: *"A local grocery store with 12 employees is deciding whether to replace 4 cashiers with self-checkout kiosks. Should they do it? Do NOT give a quick yes/no answer. First, think step by step through: (1) Customer adoption barriers, (2) Upfront costs vs. labor savings, and (3) Risk of theft. Then provide a final recommendation."*
  3. **Step 3:** Read through ChatGPT's intermediate reasoning steps carefully.
  4. **Step 4:** Identify at least one hidden or risky business assumption made in each step, and write it into your Step-by-Step Logic Table.
- **What to Expect:** You will see the AI's thought process clearly, making it easy to spot good ideas and unrealistic assumptions.
- **Exercise (Step-by-Step Logic Table):** Fill out a 3-column table: AI Reasoning Step | Business Assumption | Student Check (*Sound* or *Risky?*).

### Module 2: Business Data, Evaluation & Project Planning

#### Session 2.1: Making Sense of Business Data
- **Duration:** 3 Hours (180 minutes)
  - *Hour 1 (50 min):* Comprehensive Concept Lecture & Live Demonstration
  - *Hour 2 (50 min):* Guided Hands-On Lab (Chipotle order revenue analysis & verification)
  - *Hour 3 (50 min):* Structured Worksheet Exercise (Data Check Table)
- **Goal:** Paste simple tables and numbers into ChatGPT to get summaries, while checking for math and data errors.
- **Lecture Content (Hour 1 — 50 Minutes):**
  - **Part 1: How LLMs Ingest and Process Tabular Data (15 min):**  
    Explain how to input tables into ChatGPT using CSV (comma-separated values), TSV (tab-separated values), or Markdown tables. Teach students that LLMs parse data row by row, converting text cells into tokens. Clean table headers are critical: ambiguous column names lead to misread data.
  - **Part 2: Why AI Struggles with Arithmetic — The Token Problem (15 min):**  
    LLMs do not have an internal math calculation chip like Excel; they predict numbers based on statistical language patterns. A number like `$1,429.50` might be broken into three separate tokens (`$1`, `429`, `.50`). When adding 20 numbers together, ChatGPT frequently makes calculation errors while explaining the math with total confidence.
  - **Part 3: The Golden Rule of Business Data Analytics (10 min):**  
    - *What AI is Great At:* Spotting qualitative patterns, identifying top-performing categories, summarizing customer order notes, and drafting executive briefings.
    - *What Humans / Spreadsheets Must Do:* Calculating revenue sums, profit margins, tax percentages, and financial audits.
  - **Part 4: Instructor Live Demonstration Script & Discussion (10 min):**  
    *Live Demo:* Paste 15 rows of customer order data from the Chipotle TSV dataset into ChatGPT. Ask it to calculate total revenue. Open a calculator on screen and add the numbers manually. Show students the discrepancy (e.g., AI calculation off by $12.50) to demonstrate why blind trust in AI arithmetic is dangerous.  
    *Discussion Question:* *"What instruction can you add to a prompt to improve ChatGPT's numerical accuracy?"* (Answer: Instruct it to *"Show your formula and calculation step-by-step for each row before stating the total"*).
- **Public Data Source:** [Chipotle Restaurant Sales & Orders Dataset (TSV)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/justmarkham/DAT8/master/data/chipotle.tsv) — Real customer orders with quantities, menu items, and item prices.
- **How to Practice:**
  1. **Step 1:** Open the Chipotle orders dataset link. Highlight and copy the header row plus rows 1 to 20.
  2. **Step 2:** Paste the copied rows into ChatGPT and enter: *"Here is a sample of restaurant orders. Please: (1) Calculate the total dollar revenue from these rows, (2) Name the top 2 most ordered items, and (3) Identify the most expensive single item."*
  3. **Step 3:** Open a calculator or spreadsheet and manually calculate the true total revenue from the raw numbers.
  4. **Step 4:** Compare ChatGPT's answers with your manual calculation. Mark each statement as *Correct*, *Incorrect Math*, or *Made Up* in your Data Check Table.
- **What to Expect:** Learn that AI can summarize text well, but can sometimes calculate numbers incorrectly or misread table cells.
- **Exercise (Data Check Table):** Compare 3 statements made by ChatGPT against the original table numbers, marking each as *Correct*, *Incorrect Math*, or *Made Up*.

#### Session 2.2: Quality Control & Improving AI Results
- **Duration:** 3 Hours (180 minutes)
  - *Hour 1 (50 min):* Comprehensive Concept Lecture & Live Demonstration
  - *Hour 2 (50 min):* Guided Hands-On Lab (Improving customer churn analysis from v1 to v2)
  - *Hour 3 (50 min):* Structured Worksheet Exercise (Prompt Improvement Log)
- **Goal:** Apply a disciplined, iterative prompt-engineering method to systematically upgrade output quality.
- **Lecture Content (Hour 1 — 50 Minutes):**
  - **Part 1: The Iterative Engineering Mindset (15 min):**  
    In professional business environments, prompt engineering is an iterative software process, not magic. You almost never use your first prompt. You draft Prompt v1.0, test it, analyze the output's flaws, modify the prompt variables, and evaluate Prompt v2.0.
  - **Part 2: Diagnosing Output Weaknesses (15 min):**  
    Teach students to categorize prompt failures into 4 specific buckets:
    1. *Vagueness Flaw:* Output gives generic platitudes instead of concrete advice. (Fix: Add domain context).
    2. *Hallucination Flaw:* Output invents facts or numbers. (Fix: Constrain to provided text only).
    3. *Formatting Flaw:* Output produces unstructured walls of text. (Fix: Add schema template).
    4. *Tone Flaw:* Output is overly casual or academic. (Fix: Set explicit executive persona).
  - **Part 3: Documenting Prompt Deltas (10 min):**  
    How corporate AI teams maintain "Prompt Change Logs" (documenting what rule was changed and how much it improved output scores) to track business ROI.
  - **Part 4: Instructor Live Demonstration Script & Discussion (10 min):**  
    *Live Demo:* Paste 10 rows of customer churn data into ChatGPT.  
    *Test 1 (v1.0):* `"Why are customers leaving?"` (Produces generic textbook marketing ideas).  
    *Diagnosis:* AI failed to analyze the `Contract` and `MonthlyCharges` columns.  
    *Test 2 (v2.0):* `"Compare month-to-month contracts vs. annual contracts in a 3-column table; focus specifically on customers paying over $70/month."` Show the dramatic improvement in actionable business insights.  
    *Discussion Question:* *"Why is keeping an iteration log more valuable than simply rewriting a prompt until it works?"* (Answer: It documents what prompt levers actually drove the improvement, allowing team-wide reusability).
- **Public Data Source:** [Customer Churn & Service Contract Dataset (CSV)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pycaret/pycaret/master/datasets/churn.csv) — Real customer service data showing monthly charges, contract types, and service cancellations.
- **How to Practice:**
  1. **Step 1:** Open the dataset link and copy 10 rows of customer data (including tenure, contract type, and monthly charges).
  2. **Step 2 (Prompt v1.0):** Paste the 10 rows into ChatGPT and ask: *"Why are customers leaving?"* Review the output and notice how general and unhelpful the advice is.
  3. **Step 3 (Prompt v2.0):** Diagnose what was missing. Write an improved prompt: *"Analyze the 'Contract' and 'MonthlyCharges' columns in the 10 rows above. Compare the cancellation rate between 'Month-to-month' and 'One year' customers. Present the findings in a 3-column table and suggest 2 specific discounts to keep month-to-month customers."*
  4. **Step 4:** Run Prompt v2.0. Compare the quality difference and document your changes in the Prompt Improvement Log.
- **What to Expect:** See how small, specific prompt changes directly improve the quality of the answer.
- **Exercise (Prompt Improvement Log):** Fill out a 4-part sheet: Original Prompt -> What Was Wrong -> Changes Made -> Improvement Rating (1 to 5).

#### Session 2.3: Scoping Projects & Setting Goals
- **Duration:** 3 Hours (180 minutes)
  - *Hour 1 (50 min):* Comprehensive Concept Lecture & Live Demonstration
  - *Hour 2 (50 min):* Guided Hands-On Lab (Interactive project charter scoping dialogue)
  - *Hour 3 (50 min):* Structured Worksheet Exercise (Project Scope Grid)
- **Goal:** Use ChatGPT as a project management partner to define clear project charters and boundaries.
- **Lecture Content (Hour 1 — 50 Minutes):**
  - **Part 1: Why Business Projects Fail — Scope Creep (15 min):**  
    Over 50% of corporate projects fail or go over budget due to "scope creep" — the gradual, uncontrolled expansion of project requirements without extra time or funding. A well-defined Project Charter establishes hard boundaries before work begins.
  - **Part 2: The 4 Pillars of a Project Charter (15 min):**  
    - **In-Scope:** The explicit tasks, deliverables, and features the team commits to deliver.
    - **Out-of-Scope:** Explicitly documented exclusions (e.g., *"We will build the website, but will NOT create custom video content"*).
    - **SMART Deliverables:** Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound milestones.
    - **Dependencies & Critical Risks:** Factors outside the team's control (e.g., supplier permits, software integrations).
  - **Part 3: The Consultative Dialogue Technique (10 min):**  
    Instead of asking AI to write a whole plan at once, instruct it: *"Act as a Project Management Professional (PMP). Ask me 3 questions about our timeline, budget, and constraints before proposing a charter."* This turns AI into an interactive management consultant.
  - **Part 4: Instructor Live Demonstration Script & Discussion (10 min):**  
    *Live Demo:* Conduct a live scoping session in ChatGPT for launching a student coffee cart with a $4,000 budget. Show how forcing the AI to define what is *Out-of-Scope* prevents budget failure.  
    *Discussion Question:* *"Why is the 'Out-of-Scope' section often more important for a project manager than the 'In-Scope' section?"* (Answer: It protects the team from client demands that would cause budget overruns and project failure).
- **Public Data Source:** [Project Charter Guide & Framework (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_charter) — Standard project management reference defining project scope, objectives, and boundaries.
- **How to Practice:**
  1. **Step 1:** Review the core components of a project charter (Scope, Objectives, Deliverables, Risks) on the public reference link.
  2. **Step 2:** Choose a business project scenario: *"Opening a student-run coffee kiosk on campus with a $4,000 budget and an 8-week deadline."*
  3. **Step 3:** Prompt ChatGPT: *"Act as a Senior Project Manager. Help me plan this project charter. Ask me 3 clarifying questions about our budget and staffing before creating the charter."*
  4. **Step 4:** Answer ChatGPT's 3 questions, then ask it to generate the final In-Scope and Out-of-Scope boundaries along with 3 measurable milestones. Fill out your Project Scope Grid.
- **What to Expect:** A structured project plan that clearly states what the team must deliver and what is out of scope.
- **Exercise (Project Scope Grid):** Fill in a 4-part grid: In-Scope Tasks, Out-of-Scope Tasks, 3 Measurable Milestones, and Main Project Risk.

#### Session 2.4: Market Strategy & Customer Personas
- **Duration:** 3 Hours (180 minutes)
  - *Hour 1 (50 min):* Comprehensive Concept Lecture & Live Demonstration
  - *Hour 2 (50 min):* Guided Hands-On Lab (Generating contrasting buyer personas from Pew data)
  - *Hour 3 (50 min):* Structured Worksheet Exercise (Customer Comparison Grid)
- **Goal:** Create realistic customer profiles (personas) to help design marketing and sales messages.
- **Lecture Content (Hour 1 — 50 Minutes):**
  - **Part 1: Beyond Demographics — The Jobs-to-Be-Done (JTBD) Theory (15 min):**  
    Traditional marketing relies on demographic descriptions (e.g., *"Women aged 25-35"*). Clayton Christensen's JTBD theory teaches that customers "hire" products to solve specific struggles in their daily lives. A successful persona must define emotional pain points, buying friction, and daily frustrations.
  - **Part 2: The "Clone Persona" Trap in AI (15 min):**  
    When asked to generate personas, ChatGPT often creates generic characters with different names who have the exact same cheerful attitude and willingness to buy. To get useful business insights, managers must prompt for sharp contrast: one price-sensitive skeptic vs. one convenience-driven early adopter.
  - **Part 3: Connecting Personas to Marketing Hooks (10 min):**  
    How to use persona pain points to write tailored value proposition headlines that directly neutralize customer hesitations.
  - **Part 4: Instructor Live Demonstration Script & Discussion (10 min):**  
    *Live Demo:* Prompt ChatGPT for 2 personas for an electric commuter bicycle:  
    - Persona 1: A cash-strapped university student worried about bicycle theft.  
    - Persona 2: A 50-year-old suburban professional worried about arriving at work sweaty.  
    Show how the marketing messages for the two personas must be completely different.

#### Session 3.2: Operations & Process Improvement
- **Duration:** 3 Hours (180 minutes)

- **Goal:** Find operational bottlenecks and explore root causes using simple problem-solving tools.
- **Lecture Content (Hour 1 — 50 Minutes):**
  - **Part 1: Operational Bottlenecks & The Theory of Constraints (15 min):**  
    In manufacturing and service operations, Eliyahu Goldratt's Theory of Constraints states that any manageable system is limited in achieving more of its goals by at least one constraint (the bottleneck). Increasing speed anywhere other than the bottleneck is an illusion.
  - **Part 2: The Toyota 5-Whys Diagnostic Methodology (15 min):**  
    Developed by Sakichi Toyoda, the 5-Whys technique resolves operational problems by repeatedly asking "Why?" 5 times. This pushes past superficial symptoms (e.g., *"The driver was late"*) down to systemic management failures (e.g., *"The maintenance scheduling software lacked automated reminders"*).
  - **Part 3: Auditing AI Causal Reasoning (10 min):**  
    AI often makes "logical leaps" when doing root-cause analysis (e.g., jumping from a late delivery directly to *"the CEO lacks vision"*). Business analysts must audit each causal link: Is this a *Strong Link* (empirically probable), a *Logical Leap* (unproven jump), or *Superficial* (blaming individuals instead of systems)?
  - **Part 4: Instructor Live Demonstration Script & Discussion (10 min):**  
    *Live Demo:* Present an operational crisis: An e-commerce warehouse had 500 delayed shipments during Black Friday. Run a 5-Whys analysis in ChatGPT. Show how each "Why" uncovers a deeper process flaw (Barcode scanner battery failures -> No charging dock SOP).  
    *Discussion Question:* *"Why do bad managers stop at Why #1 or #2, and what are the consequences?"* (Answer: They blame employee carelessness and fire workers, but the underlying system failure remains, causing the exact same mistake to happen again).
- **Public Data Source:** [Five Whys Root Cause Analysis (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_whys) — Classic problem-solving framework with real manufacturing and operational examples.
- **How to Practice:**
  1. **Step 1:** Review the 5-Whys methodology and the classic factory breakdown example on the public Wikipedia page.
  2. **Step 2:** Create an operational problem statement: *"An online retail warehouse experienced a 3-day shipping delay for 500 customer orders during the holiday sale."*
  3. **Step 3:** In ChatGPT, enter: *"Act as an Operations Manager. Conduct a rigorous 5-Whys root cause analysis on this delay. At each 'Why' level, identify the direct operational failure and ask the next logical question until reaching the system-level root cause."*
  4. **Step 4:** Evaluate each of the 5 reasoning levels. Mark each link as a *Strong Link*, a *Weak Link*, or *Unrealistic* in your 5-Whys Diagnostic Table.
- **What to Expect:** Discover whether the AI's explanation makes practical business sense or makes illogical jumps.
- **Exercise (5-Whys Diagnostic Table):** Rate each of the 5 "Why" answers from ChatGPT as *Strong Link*, *Weak Link*, or *Unrealistic*.
